## 11. Supply and demand model

Use a transparent deterministic simulation model with seeded stochastic events.

Demand:

`Demand = BaseDemand × DemandFactor × (CurrentPrice / BasePrice)^(-DemandElasticity)`

Supply:

`Supply = BaseSupply × SupplyFactor × (CurrentPrice / BasePrice)^(SupplyElasticity)`

Market imbalance:

`Imbalance = (Demand - Supply) / max(Demand, Supply, 1)`

Inventory pressure:

`StockPressure = (TargetStock - AvailableStock) / max(TargetStock, 1)`

Combined pressure:

`MarketPressure = 0.65 × Imbalance + 0.35 × StockPressure`

Target price:

`TargetPrice = BasePrice × EventMultiplier × exp(Volatility × MarketPressure)`

Clamp the target price to configured product price boundaries.

Smoothed price:

`NewPrice = OldPrice + Alpha × (TargetPrice - OldPrice)`

Default:

`Alpha = 0.25`

All formulas must be implemented as independent, unit-tested pure functions.

### Important implementation requirement

Do not use the same supply or demand quantity multiple times in the same simulation cycle.

Define a single authoritative market tick.

For each tick:

1. Calculate planned supply.
2. Calculate planned demand.
3. Execute NPC transactions within available inventory and budgets.
4. Apply player transactions.
5. Reconcile inventory.
6. Calculate market pressure.
7. Calculate target prices.
8. Update prices.
9. Record market history.

Separate planned demand from executed demand.

Separate available stock from incoming stock.

Separate market inventory from player inventory.

This is necessary to avoid double counting.

## 12. Price discovery

Price discovery must account for:

- Current market price.
- Available inventory.
- Executed trades.
- Transaction size.
- Market liquidity.
- Buy/sell spreads.
- Product quality.
- Location-specific costs.

Buy price:

`BuyPrice = MarketPrice × (1 + BuySpread)`

Sell price:

`SellPrice = MarketPrice × (1 - SellSpread)`

Initial spreads:

- Retail market: 3%.
- Wholesale market: 1.5–2%.

Large orders may incur price impact.

`Impact = min(TradeQuantity / max(TargetStock, 1), 0.25)`

Price impact must be applied once, not both in the trade engine and again as a duplicate inventory adjustment.

# PART V – TRADING AND FINANCE

## 16. Trading engine

Support:

- Buy.
- Sell.
- Wholesale purchase.
- Wholesale sale.
- Bulk discounts.
- Negotiated prices.
- Location-based arbitrage.
- Commercial contracts.

Each transaction must validate:

- Player cash.
- Inventory.
- Storage capacity.
- Product quality.
- Quantity.
- Price.
- Transaction fees.
- Transport costs.
- Counterparty funds.

Transactions must be atomic.

If validation fails, no financial or inventory state may change.

## 17. Inventory and warehouse

Use inventory lots.

Each lot:

- Lot ID.
- Product ID.
- Quantity.
- Unit cost.
- Quality.
- Purchase time.
- Expiry time.
- Storage location.

Use FIFO by default for inventory consumption.

Allow alternative strategies only if they create a meaningful gameplay benefit.

Quality decay:

`NewQuality = OldQuality - DecayRate × DeltaTime × StorageMultiplier`

Initial storage multipliers:

- Normal storage: 1.0.
- Cool storage: 0.5.
- Refrigerated storage: 0.2.

Spoilage must affect actual inventory value.

## 18. Financial accounting

Maintain a transaction ledger.

Metrics:

- Cash.
- Revenue.
- COGS.
- Gross profit.
- Operating expenses.
- Net profit.
- Inventory value.
- Liabilities.
- Business asset value.
- Net worth.

Formulas:

`GrossProfit = Revenue - COGS`

`NetProfit = GrossProfit - OperatingExpenses`

`NetWorth = Cash + InventoryLiquidationValue + BusinessAssetValue - Liabilities`

Purchasing goods:

- Cash decreases.
- Inventory increases.
- No immediate COGS recognition.

Selling goods:

- Cash increases.
- Inventory decreases.
- Revenue increases.
- COGS is recognized based on sold inventory lots.

Maintain an immutable transaction history.

Use integer or fixed-point currency values. Avoid floating-point accumulation errors in financial balances.

## 20. NPC behavior

NPCs should act according to their economic role.

Consumer:

- Buys according to budget and needs.
- Responds to price.
- Has preferred products.

Vendor:

- Purchases wholesale.
- Sells retail.
- Manages inventory.
- Adjusts price.

Wholesaler:

- Buys larger quantities.
- Tracks price differences.
- Uses inventory targets.

Competitor:

- Responds to player pricing.
- Can discount goods.
- Can compete for suppliers.

Supplier:

- Provides goods according to stock and schedule.
- May change prices based on scarcity.

NPCs must not create unlimited money or inventory.

## 21. NPC economic simulation performance

Do not simulate every NPC every frame.

Use an event-driven or scheduled simulation.

Prioritize:

- Visible NPCs for detailed interactions.
- Background NPCs for aggregated economic activity.

Background agents may be simulated in batches.

The economic result must remain deterministic for a given seed and simulation history.
